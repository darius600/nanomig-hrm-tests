# Photographs of real machines

What the screen photographs published with vAmigaTS show about the pages of this suite, where a program reaches the condition a page asks about, and where none does. Each finding page that a photograph bears on says so under *What photographs of real machines show*; this document holds what they have in common and the whole list in one place.

## What the photographs are

[vAmigaTS](https://github.com/dirkwhoffmann/vAmigaTS/tree/e807e3256d73135f1960e7f9aef2c16d8a6a56b4) is the test suite of the vAmiga emulator. Beside each program's source it publishes, for some programs, a photograph of a real machine's screen running it. The file name is mostly `<program>_<label>.JPG`, and the label (`A500`, `A500_ECS`, `A500_OCS`, `A500_OCS5`, `ACA500_ECS_NTSC`, or none at all) is all the name says about the machine. The programs draw their result on the screen: a register read back as sixteen bars with bit 15 first, or Copper colour changes whose position along a line or down the frame is the timing. Every program and photograph this document names is at commit `e807e3256d73135f1960e7f9aef2c16d8a6a56b4`, linked at that commit.

## How they were read

- **A narrow strip.** One strip of a few columns is read per bar or per row, so the picture's uneven brightness across the screen does not enter. Brightness of a bar is measured on the strip and set beside the same photograph's lit and dark references, never beside another photograph's.
- **The scale from the program itself.** The programs draw a row of Copper dashes, each a MOVE of 4 colour clocks, so the dash period of 8 colour clocks (42 to 44 pixels, 5.4 pixels per colour clock) gives the scale in the same picture. Red separators between the bars give the bar index and the line scale.
- **Tilt.** A photograph is not square to the screen. The tilt is taken from a line the program draws horizontal and removed before positions are compared.
- **One label per file.** A photograph is cited by the label in its name and by nothing more.
- **Duplicates and sibling-named files left out.** A photograph that is byte-identical, by hash, to another program's, or filed under the name of a sibling program, does not show which program it belongs to, and carries no result. The one place such files appear, the POTGOR half of NM-HRM-038, says so and reads them conditionally.
- **The programs were not run on a machine here.** Every number is read from a photograph; the stimulus is read from the program's source, line by line, and, for the programs a result rests on most, the bytes checked in the disk image.

## What they cannot say

- **Which chip.** `A500_ECS` names a machine, not a Denise: an ECS-named A500 may carry an OCS Denise. `OCS` and `OCS5` are different labels, and nothing shows them to be one machine. No label names a chip revision.
- **One exposure each.** Brightness cannot be compared between two photographs of different programs; the exposure differs.
- **No absolute position.** The scaler and the crop of the screen move and cut the picture; positions are differences within a photograph, and the picture's edge is not the chip's.
- **No device on a port.** What was plugged into a joystick, serial or parallel port is not recorded.
- **Not every bit of a register.** A bar is lit or dark; a count is a position along a line.
