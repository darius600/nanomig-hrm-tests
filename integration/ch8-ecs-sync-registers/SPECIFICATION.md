# Specification: ECS programmable horizontal beam and sync registers

This document specifies one integration test package for the Enhanced Chip
Set registers HTOTAL, HSSTRT and HSSTOP. It was written from the package's
claims (`claims-spec-stage.jsonl`) and the pages of the *Amiga Hardware
Reference Manual*, 3rd edition, by an author who never saw the
implementation or any test. Page numbers are printed page numbers. The
register rows and the pages this package rests on (p.23, p.194, p.195,
p.216, p.260, p.264, p.266, p.280, p.285, p.298, p.299, p.304 and p.305) were
read on the printed scan. A builder implements the package from this document
and does not change what it specifies.

## 1. Claims without a proposition

| Claim | Subject | Why no proposition |
|---|---|---|
| `opus:8-appA-oos.c1` | HBSTOP, 1C6, "Horizontal line position for HBLANK stop" | No page names the condition under which the programmed position takes effect, and no page says what HBLANK does to the outputs a test sees. Details below. |
| `opus:8-appA-oos.c2` | HBSTRT, 1C4, "Horizontal line position for HBLANK start" | As c1. |
| `opus:8-appA-oos.c3` | HCENTER, 1E2, "Horizontal position for Vsync on interlace" | No enabling condition, no statement of which field or which VSYNC edge it positions, and the effect lies in the sub-line timing of VSYNC, which the test sees only after the line doubler. Details below. |
| `opus:8-display.c7` | p.260, "RGB Monitors ("analog RGB")" | The claim states the name and kind of a connector output. The test sees digital 4-bit R, G, B values and separate H and V sync, not the connector. Details below. |
| `opus:8-appA-oos.c4` | HSSTOP, 1C2, "Horizontal line position for HSYNC stop" | The claim bundles write-only, Agnus, ECS-mark and the address with the stop-position functional effect; only the functional effect is testable. Details below. |
| `opus:8-appA-oos.c5` | HSSTRT, 1DE, "Horizontal sync start (VARHSY)" | As c4, for the start-position effect. Details below. |
| `opus:8-appA-oos.c6` | HTOTAL, 1C0, "Highest number count, horiz. line (VARBEAMEN=1)" | As c4, for the highest-count effect. Details below. |

**HBSTRT, HBSTOP (c2, c1).** p.304: "Programmable blanking is implemented
through four new ECS Agnus registers: HBSTRT ... Horizontal line position
for HBLANK start, HBSTOP ... Horizontal line position for HBLANK stop".

- No page says when these positions take effect. On p.280 HTOTAL carries
  "(VARBEAMEN=1)" and HSSTRT carries "(VARHSY)". The HBSTRT and HBSTOP rows
  on p.280, p.293 and p.298 carry no condition. None of the BEAMCON0 bits on
  p.305 names horizontal blanking. HARDDIS is "Disable hardwired
  vertical/horizontal blank", VARVBEN is "Use VBSTRT/STOP disable hard
  window stop" (vertical only), BLANKEN is "Composite blank redirection" and
  VARBEAMEN is "Variable beam counter comparator enable". Any choice of bits
  would be invented. The pages' silence does not mean the registers act
  unconditionally.
- No page says what HBLANK does to R, G or B. p.80 says only "horizontal
  blanking actually limits the displayable video to 368 low resolution
  pixels". p.23 puts "Horizontal blanking ... in the range of $0F to $35"
  inside "an unused horizontal portion of $04 to $47 (during which only the
  background color is displayed)". The test has no blanking output of its
  own.

**HCENTER (c3).** p.304 lists it among "five new enhanced Agnus registers"
that implement programmable synchronization, but:

- p.305 has separate VARVSYEN and VARHSYEN bits, and neither page says which
  of them, if either, governs HCENTER.
- No page says which field of an interlaced frame, or which VSYNC edge,
  HCENTER positions. The only relation given is advice: "A reasonable
  composite can be generated by setting HCENTER half a horizontal line from
  HSSTRT" (p.304). That says how to choose values, not what the output does.
- The effect would be the horizontal position of a VSYNC edge within a line.
  The test sees VSYNC only after the line doubler, which re-times the chip's
  lines.

**Analog RGB (c7).** The claim says that the first kind of RGB video on the
23-pin connector is for RGB monitors and is called "analog RGB". A name and a
kind of connector output have no consequence at the outputs a test sees.

- The quote's "Provides four outputs; Red (R), Green (G), Blue (B), and Sync
  (S)" describes connector pins, and p.319 lists three sync pins (CSYNC\*,
  HSYNC\*, VSYNC\*), not one.
- The quote's "up to 4,096 different colors on-screen simultaneously"
  names no display mode that achieves it. Sixteen levels per gun is fixed by
  the 4-bit outputs the harness provides, so a check of it would test the
  harness, not the chips.

**Parts of c4, c5 and c6 that have no proposition of their own.**

- "W" (write-only) gives no expected read value. The pages forbid the read:
  "If a register is marked as a write-only register, do not attempt to read
  from it, as this may trash the register and crash the system." (p.263 to
  p.264). The programs below never read these registers.
- "A" (Agnus) cannot be seen separately at the outputs.
- "(E)" makes ECS a precondition (PC-ID below). No page says what the
  original chips do with a write to 1C0, 1C2, 1DC or 1DE. p.298 marks all of
  them "new", so an OCS run has no expected value and is not graded.
- The address is checked by every proposition, because every stimulus writes
  exactly `$DFF000` plus the listed address (p.263: "680x0 address = (chip
  address) + $DFF000").
- The claims' remark "This is a display register, not part of the disk
  register group" is about where the row is printed. Nothing observable
  follows from it.

Every non-functional part of c4, c5 and c6 is conceded above, and no page
separates the functional clause from these others within one claim: none of
the three ids establishes the claim it bundles, so c4, c5 and c6 are
withdrawn in full (recorded, not asserted, in the test). Each leaves an
unlabelled, page-derived functional proposition: P1/P2 (c6, HTOTAL's
highest-count effect), P3 (c5, HSSTRT's start-position effect) and P4 (c4,
HSSTOP's stop-position effect).

c4 and c5 each also say which edge of the sync pulse the register sets (sync
stop, sync start). P3 and P4 cannot show that, and this is a stated limit of
the package: both grade a move relative to the picture (the landmark M of
P3 point 3), and the video output the test sees has passed a line doubler
that re-times the chip's lines (as for HCENTER above), so no page-anchored
reference for which edge moved survives to the boundary the test sees. The
ids therefore stay on the RECORDED lines of c4 and c5, with this reason, and
on no graded line.

## 2. Page notes

1. **Page number.** Claims c1 to c6 record p.279. On the printed scan, p.279
   ends with DSKSYNC. The six rows HBSTOP, HBSTRT, HCENTER, HSSTOP, HSSTRT
   and HTOTAL are printed at the top of **p.280**, with the same wording as
   the claims. This specification cites p.280.
2. **HTOTAL: highest count or number of increments.** p.280 and p.298 say
   "Highest number count, horiz. line". p.23 says the counter starts at $0
   ("The horizontal beam position has a value of $0 to $E2"), so a highest
   count N means a line of N+1 counts. p.304 says instead: "The value in this
   register represents the number of 280ns increments on the horizontal
   line", which reads as N. Only what both readings agree on is specified: no
   count exceeds N, the line lasts N or N+1 counts, and a change of HTOTAL by
   d changes the line by exactly d counts.
3. **Standard PAL line.** p.23: "In PAL, they are all 227 long." p.304:
   "PAL (625 lines, 227.5 colorclocks per scan line)". p.194 gives "227.5
   'color clocks'" per horizontal line in general. The HTOTAL runs therefore
   set LOLDIS ("Disable long line/short line toggle", p.305), so every page
   predicts lines of one constant length. The standard length is used only
   as a unit check that accepts 227 or 228.
4. **p.304 misprint.** The HTOTAL bit row is printed "15 14 13 12 11 10 09
   08 07 06 05 04 03 02 10 00", with "10" for "01". The field h8 to h1 in
   bits 7 to 0 is unaffected.
5. **BEAMCON0 function text.** p.266 and p.293 say "(SHRES,PAL)" and p.298
   says "(SHRES,UHRES,PAL)". The bit table on p.305 has no bit named SHRES
   or UHRES. Only the bits p.305 defines are used here (bits 5, 7, 8 and 11).
6. **BEAMCON0 warning.** p.305: "Do not write to this register. Incorrectly
   writing directly to BEAMCON0 has the (remote) possibility of destroying
   your multisync monitor." The function of every register in this package
   depends on BEAMCON0 bits (p.280: "(VARBEAMEN=1)", "(VARHSY)"). The
   warning is addressed to graphics.library users, and the test drives no
   monitor, so the test writes BEAMCON0.
7. **Bit layout of HSSTRT and HSSTOP.** The pages give none. HTOTAL's layout
   (bits 15 to 8 zero, h8 to h1 in bits 7 to 0, p.304) is the only
   horizontal-position layout given for these registers. The advice on p.304
   ("HBSTOP at (HSSTOP-HSSTRT) before HCENTER") implies that the positions
   share one linear scale, but it gives neither the scale nor the direction.
   Values here are written in bits 7 to 0 with bits 15 to 8 zero, and only
   "moves" or "does not move" -- graded as exact inequality or exact
   equality, never by how much or which way -- is graded (section 6).
8. **Enable for HSSTRT and HSSTOP.** p.280, p.293 and p.298 name VARHSY
   (VARHSYEN) as HSSTRT's condition and do not say whether VARBEAMEN is
   also needed. HSSTOP carries no condition in any register table and is
   tied to HSSTRT only because p.304 groups it with HSSTRT under
   "Programmable synchronization is implemented through five new enhanced
   Agnus registers" and p.305 names bit 8 "Variable horizontal sync
   enable". Both bits are therefore set: VARHSYEN and VARBEAMEN together
   are the only setting every reading of p.280, p.298 and p.305 agrees
   predicts a move (section 7, "P3, P4"). VARHSYEN without VARBEAMEN has
   no page-predicted outcome and is not part of this package's stimulus.
9. **Chip ID radix.** p.299 prints the VPOSR chip identification values as
   "10", "00", "20" and "30" with no "$", while p.21 says "Hexadecimal
   numbers are distinguished from decimal numbers by the $ prefix."
   Precondition PC-ID accepts both readings.
10. **p.260 output counts.** The digital RGB bullet says "Provides four
    outputs ... named Red (R), Green (G), Blue (B), Half-Intensity (I), and
    Sync (S)", which is five names. The analog bullet's single "Sync (S)"
    stands against three sync pins on p.319. Neither affects this package.

## 3. Conventions for every run

**Harness.** ECS selected, PAL selected, 2 MB of Chip memory. Every run below
is made once with each CPU core, and the expected results are the same for
both cores.

**Register access.**

- Every custom register is written at `$DFF000` plus its address (p.263).
  Word registers are written with `MOVE.W`. Pointer pairs are written with
  one `MOVE.L` (p.263: "These registers may be written with one MOVE.L
  instruction").
- The program never reads HTOTAL, VTOTAL, HSSTRT, HSSTOP or BEAMCON0, and
  never applies `CLR`, `BSET`, `OR` or any other read-modify-write
  instruction to them (p.263 to p.264).
- Bits that a register's page calls unused are written 0 (p.264).

Addresses used (Appendix B, p.287 to p.293; Appendix C, p.298):

| Register | Address | Register | Address |
|---|---|---|---|
| VPOSR | 004 | DIWSTRT | 08E |
| VHPOSR | 006 | DIWSTOP | 090 |
| COP1LCH/L | 080/082 | DDFSTRT | 092 |
| COPJMP1 | 088 | DDFSTOP | 094 |
| DMACON | 096 | BPL1PTH/L | 0E0/0E2 |
| INTENA | 09A | BPLCON0/1/2 | 100/102/104 |
| COLOR00/01 | 180/182 | BPL1MOD | 108 |
| HTOTAL | 1C0 | HSSTOP | 1C2 |
| VTOTAL | 1C8 | BEAMCON0 | 1DC |
| HSSTRT | 1DE | | |

**Program start.** Every program begins with `INTENA ← $7FFF` (all enables
cleared, p.280) and `DMACON ← $07FF`. The DMACON write clears bits 10 to 0
and keeps bits 12 and 11 zero, which p.278 marks "X".

**Markers and DONE.** MARK1 and MARK2 are two words in Chip memory at
addresses the builder chooses. The program writes `$A5A5` to MARK1 and
`$5A5A` to MARK2 with `MOVE.W`. The test finds each marker as the CPU write
of that data to that address at the Chip-memory port, and its time. After
the measured work, every program executes the `DONE` macro
(`integration/harness/nanomig.i`): it stores a fixed value at a fixed
address, then holds. The test finds this write too, and every capture
asserts it, distinguishing a red that comes from the clause under test from
one that comes from a program that never finished.

**Units.**

- A *color clock* is one memory access cycle: "there are 227.5 'color
  clocks', or memory access cycles. A memory cycle is approximately 280 ns in
  duration." (p.194). The builder converts the port's system-clock times
  into color clocks with a ratio neither the page nor the harness header
  defines; it is read off the implementation (section 7) and is checked at
  runtime by anchor A3 below, which requires every graded period -- not
  only the first -- to convert exactly (no remainder) to 227 or 228, the
  page's own PAL line length. Every other check that consumes this ratio
  (P1-COVER, P2-LINES, A2, P2(a), P2(b), P2(c), P2-CONTRAST and A5-REF)
  records "precondition not met" unless both A3 lines hold.
- An *output pixel clock* is the video output's sample clock.
- Horizontal counts written to HTOTAL and read from VHPOSR are in the same
  unit. HTOTAL uses "h8 h7 h6 h5 h4 h3 h2 h1" in bits 7 to 0 (p.304).
  VHPOSR bits 7 to 0 are "The horizontal position, bits H8-H1" (p.216),
  "RESOLUTION = 1/160 of screen width (280 ns)" (p.285).

**Shared preconditions.** Every graded run asserts these first. Every graded
line of a run is gated on that run's own PC-ID and PC-MARK, and names every
other precondition it consumes; a line whose gate fails reports "precondition
not met" with the names of the preconditions that failed, and is neither
green nor red. A contrast turns red only the line it targets: the lines gated
on it report "precondition not met", and every other precondition stays green.

- **FIXTURE (a program-stored constant reads back).** Program H stores the
  constant `$C3A5` to a word FIXCONST in Chip memory before it reads anything
  back, and the test reads the word back with the harness's `peek_logical()`
  call, the same call that reads ID. It is asserted first, so every later read
  through that call rests on it. It stores nothing the markers or the samples
  depend on.
  - Every PC-ID line, in both groups, reads its ID through that call and is
    gated on FIXTURE.
  - *Contrast:* the same program storing `$DEAD` instead. FIXTURE fails; the
    markers, the samples and DONE are the normal run's, and every PC-ID line
    reports "precondition not met".
- **PC-ID (ECS and PAL).** The program's first register read is `VPOSR`,
  stored to a Chip-memory word ID before any BEAMCON0 write. Bits 14 to 8
  (I6 to I0) must hold the value p.299 gives for the enhanced Agnus in PAL:
  "8368 (hr) or 8372 (fat-hr) = 20 for PAL, 30 for NTSC". Because of page
  note 9, both $20 and $14 (decimal 20) are accepted.
  - *Contrast (OCS):* the same program with the harness set to OCS must
    read the original PAL value "00" and fail PC-ID.
  - *Contrast (NTSC):* the same program with the harness set to NTSC must
    read "30" ($30 or $1E) and fail PC-ID.
- **PC-MARK (program reached its register writes).** MARK1 appears at the
  port. Where MARK2 is defined, it appears later than MARK1.
  - *Contrast (MARK1):* the same program with MARK1's own write omitted. No
    marker appears, PC-MARK fails, and the rest of the program -- including
    DONE -- still runs: an honest mutation targets the one clause under
    test, not the whole program.
  - *Contrast (MARK2 later):* the same program with MARK2's write omitted.
    MARK1 appears and nothing follows it, PC-MARK fails through its second
    clause alone, and the rest of the program still runs.

## 4. Programs

### Program H (used by P1 and P2)

1. `INTENA ← $7FFF`, `DMACON ← $07FF`, then `BPLCON0 ← $0000`. BPLCON0
   clears LPEN, so VHPOSR reads the beam and not the light pen (p.216, p.271).
2. Store `$C3A5` to FIXCONST (FIXTURE, section 3), then read `VPOSR` and
   store it to ID.
3. `HTOTAL ← N`, then `VTOTAL ← $0138`.
   - p.298 marks VTOTAL "(VARBEAMEN=1)" as well, so VARBEAMEN also brings
     VTOTAL into effect. p.304 says "VTOTAL contains the line number at
     which to reset the vertical position counter".
   - $0138 = 312 keeps a field of standard PAL size (p.216: "PAL line counts
     (313)"). Nothing vertical is graded. The pages state no reset values,
     so the program writes every register it relies on.
4. `BEAMCON0 ← B`. The value registers are written before the enable, so
   the enabled comparator never uses an unprogrammed value. The pages
   prescribe no order.
5. Write MARK1.
6. `A1 ← $DFF006`, `A0 ← SAMPLES`. Run a block of 64 `MOVE.W (A1),(A0)+`
   instructions 256 times with a `DBRA` loop: 16384 samples, 32 KB in Chip
   memory.
7. Write MARK2, then `DONE`.

Every DMA channel stays off, so the only DMA accesses at the port are memory
refresh (p.194). The run lasts until MARK2 has been written.

### Program S (used by P3 and P4)

1. `INTENA ← $7FFF`, `DMACON ← $07FF`.
2. Read `VPOSR` and store it to ID.
3. Set up a basic low-resolution playfield in the steps of p.63 to p.64.
   The window and fetch values are the normal PAL values from p.59 and p.60:
   - `COLOR00 ← $0000`, `COLOR01 ← $0FFF`.
   - `BPLCON0 ← $1000` (one bitplane, low resolution, no interlace),
     `BPLCON1 ← $0000`, `BPLCON2 ← $0000`, `BPL1MOD ← $0000`.
   - `DIWSTRT ← $2C81`, `DIWSTOP ← $2CC1`, `DDFSTRT ← $0038`,
     `DDFSTOP ← $00D0`.
   - DIWHIGH is not written, so "the old scheme for DIWSTRT and DIWSTOP
     ... holds" (p.306).
4. Redisplay by Copper (p.63 step 6), started as p.30 prescribes:
   `COP1LC ← COPLIST` (one `MOVE.L`), then `MOVE.W COPJMP1,D0` (the strobe,
   "Causes copper to load PC from COP1LC"). Only then
   `DMACON ← $8380` (SET, DMAEN, BPLEN, COPEN; p.30, p.278).
5. `HSSTRT ← s`, then `HSSTOP ← e`.
6. `HTOTAL ← $00E2`, then `VTOTAL ← $0138`, before BEAMCON0: B sets
   VARBEAMEN in every S run, and HTOTAL and VTOTAL each carry "(VARBEAMEN=1)" (p.280,
   p.298), so the enabled comparators must not use an unprogrammed value
   -- the same reasoning as Program H's own HTOTAL/VTOTAL writes. $00E2 is
   "The horizontal position has a maximum value of $E2" (p.23); $0138 =
   312 is Program H's own PAL field size (p.216, "PAL line counts (313)").
7. `BEAMCON0 ← B`.
8. Write MARK1, then `DONE`.

Data in Chip memory:

- **COPLIST:** `$00E0,hi(PLANE)`, `$00E2,lo(PLANE)`, `$FFFF,$FFFE`. These
  are MOVE instructions per p.21, and the last WAIT never completes, per
  p.30.
- **PLANE:** 256 lines of 20 words each: 14 words of `$0000` followed by 6
  words of `$FFFF`.

Each displayed line is black up to the start of data word 14, then white up
to the window stop.

- Word 14 starts at low-resolution position $81 + 14·16 = $161, which is
  about horizontal count $B0 (p.60: display-window resolution is "twice the
  hardware resolution of data fetch").
- The white run is 96 low-resolution pixels, ending at the window stop $1C1
  (p.59).

The run lasts until the third output VSYNC after MARK1 has ended. Bitplane
and Copper DMA keep producing video after the CPU reaches DONE, since
neither depends on further CPU activity.

## 6. Propositions

### P1. With VARBEAMEN set, no horizontal position read back exceeds HTOTAL

1. **Proposition.** While BEAMCON0 bit 7 (VARBEAMEN) is 1, no
   horizontal-position value read back from VHPOSR exceeds the value
   written to HTOTAL. p.280, with p.298, p.304 and p.305; `opus:8-appA-oos.c6`
   bundles this functional clause with address, write-only, Agnus and
   ECS-mark clauses section 1 declines, so no id is carried on this
   assertion (recorded, section 1).
2. **Stimulus.** Program H, runs H1 and H2.
3. **Observation.** The 16384 words at SAMPLES, bits 7 to 0 (H), read from
   memory after MARK2.
   - A *wrap* is a sample whose H is smaller than the H of the sample
     before it.
   - The page's condition "(VARBEAMEN=1)" holds from the BEAMCON0 write,
     which precedes MARK1 and therefore every sample. The line in progress
     at that write may still end at the old limit.
   - Graded samples are those after the second wrap and up to the last
     sample.
4. **Expected.** Every graded sample has H ≤ N: ≤ $B0 in H1 and ≤ $C0 in
   H2. Source: "HTOTAL 1C0 W A(E) Highest number count, horiz. line
   (VARBEAMEN=1)" (p.280). p.304 describes the same field as "Highest number
   count in horizontal line" and as "the number of 280ns increments on the
   horizontal line". Under either reading, no count exceeds N (page note 2).
   The quantifier is "highest", so every graded sample -- every actual
   readback, not every color-clock instant of the counter's own value -- is
   checked, over at least 320 lines. That is more than one PAL field of 313
   lines (p.216), so the graded lines include a field boundary and vertical
   blanking lines.
5. **Preconditions.**
   - PC-ID and PC-MARK of the run.
   - **P1-LINES [H1], [H2], [H1c], [H2c]:** the graded samples of the run
     contain at least 320 wraps. The contrast runs' lines gate their
     contrasts.
   - **P1-COVER [H1], P1-COVER [H2]:** consecutive sample stores at the
     port in the graded range are never more than 16 color clocks apart
     (compared as raw clk_sys cycles against 16 × the clk_sys-per-CCK
     ratio, not after integer-dividing first; this consumes kCck and
     records "precondition not met" unless both A3 lines hold), and
     at least one graded sample has H ≥ N − 16, for each run. Together
     these show that the sampling reached across the whole line, including
     its end: they establish that no *readback* was missed, which is the
     quantifier the proposition states (point 4). A gapless observation of
     the counter's own value between readbacks is not achievable at this
     boundary -- a CPU register read cannot occur every color clock -- so
     P1-COVER is not read as proving that no unsampled instant exceeded N,
     only that the readbacks it did take cover the line.
6. **Contrasting cases.**
   - Runs H1c and H2c (VARBEAMEN = 0, all else equal). Here the counter
     runs its standard course, "a value of $0 to $E2" (p.23). At least one
     graded sample must have H > N: > $B0 in H1c and > $C0 in H2c. These
     two lines are P1-CONTRAST [H1c] and P1-CONTRAST [H2c]; each is gated
     on its own run's PC-ID, PC-MARK and P1-LINES and on nothing else.
   - For the preconditions: the X-SHORT runs fail P1-LINES, the X-BURST runs
     fail P1-COVER, and X-OCS, X-NTSC, X-HALT-H and X-NOMARK2 fail the shared
     ones.
7. **Reference frame.** The H field of VHPOSR (bits 7 to 0, H8 to H1) is
   compared with HTOTAL's field (bits 7 to 0, h8 to h1). The bit names are
   the same on p.216, p.285 and p.304.
   - **Anchor A1:** in H1c and H2c, the largest graded H lies between
     $E2 − 16 and $E3. p.23 gives $E2. With LOLDIS set, a 228-count line
     would reach $E3 (page note 3). A1 is gated on its run's PC-ID, PC-MARK
     and P1-LINES and gates no other line.

### P2. With VARBEAMEN set, every line lasts N or N+1 color clocks, and HTOTAL changes it count for count

1. **Proposition.** While VARBEAMEN is 1, every horizontal line has the
   same length, that length is N or N+1 color clocks for HTOTAL = N, and
   changing HTOTAL by d changes the length by exactly d. p.280, with p.304
   and p.305; `opus:8-appA-oos.c6` is withdrawn as in P1 (section 1), so no
   id is carried by any assertion below. No one graded line establishes the
   whole proposition: P2(a) [H1], P2(a) [H2], P2(b) and P2(c) each grade one
   clause, and the conjunction line below the four is the whole proposition,
   also unlabelled.
2. **Stimulus.** Program H, runs H1 and H2, the same runs as P1.
3. **Observation.** Refresh accesses at the Chip-memory port. With every DMA
   channel off, every DMA access is a refresh access.
   - A *refresh group* is a maximal set of refresh accesses whose
     neighbours are less than 32 color clocks apart.
   - A *line period* is the distance in color clocks between the first
     accesses of two consecutive groups.
   - Graded periods run from the second group that starts after MARK1 to
     the last group that starts before MARK2. The group in progress when
     BEAMCON0 was written is thereby excluded.
4. **Expected.**
   - (a) In each run, every graded period has one and the same value: T1
     in H1, T2 in H2. (P2(a) [H1], P2(a) [H2].)
   - (b) T2 − T1 = $C0 − $B0 = 16. (P2(b).)
   - (c) T1 − $B0 is 0 or 1. (P2(c).)
   - The conjunction of (a) for both runs, (b) and (c) is the whole
     proposition for the one change the runs make, HTOTAL $B0 to $C0
     (d = 16); other values of d are not run.

   Sources: "Highest number count, horiz. line (VARBEAMEN=1)" (p.280),
   together with p.23's count from $0, gives N+1. "The value in this
   register represents the number of 280ns increments on the horizontal
   line" (p.304) gives N. The pages disagree by one (page note 2), so (c)
   accepts both values, and (b) holds under both. Their unit is the 280 ns
   color clock (p.304, p.194).

   The quantifier is "every line": all graded periods are checked, at least
   320, which crosses a field boundary. LOLDIS is set so that no page
   predicts alternating lengths (page note 3).
5. **Preconditions.**
   - PC-ID and PC-MARK of the run.
   - **P2-LINES [H1], [H2], [H1c], [H2c]:** at least 320 graded periods in
     the run. Consumes kCck (via the 32-CCK refresh grouping); records
     "precondition not met" unless both A3 lines hold. The contrast runs'
     lines gate P2-CONTRAST and A2 [H1c], A2 [H2c].
   - **A2 [H1], [H2]** (P2(a), P2(b), P2(c), P2) and **A2 [H1c], [H2c]**
     (P2-CONTRAST).
6. **Contrasting cases.**
   - Runs H1c and H2c. With VARBEAMEN = 0, the page's condition for HTOTAL
     does not hold. Every graded period in H1c equals every graded period in
     H2c, so the HTOTAL value has no effect (P2-CONTRAST).
   - For the preconditions: the X-LATE runs fail P2-LINES, and X-OCS,
     X-NTSC, X-HALT-H and X-NOMARK2 fail the shared ones.
7. **Reference frame.** The line start is the first refresh access of a
   group. p.194: "The time-slot allocation per horizontal line is: 4 cycles
   for memory refresh". p.285: "There are four refresh time slots". Figure
   6-9 (p.195) draws the refresh slots as the first allocation of the line
   ("MEMORY REFRESH DMA TIME" from $0). Its footnote says the chart has been
   "adjusted", so only one group per line is used, not the group's exact
   position.
   - **Anchor A2** (one line per H run): every graded period contains
     exactly 4 refresh accesses. Gated on the run's PC-ID, PC-MARK and
     P2-LINES (hence on both A3 lines). It gates P2(a), P2(b), P2(c), P2 and
     P2-CONTRAST for the runs each of them reads.
   - **Anchor A3** (A3 [H1c], A3 [H2c]): in the run, every graded period --
     not only the first -- is exactly divisible by kCck (the clk_sys-per-CCK
     ratio) and converts to 227 or 228 color clocks. p.23 gives 227. p.304's
     average of 227.5 can only become 227 or 228 once the toggle is
     disabled. This is the conversion anchor every other kCck-consuming
     check (P1-COVER, P2-LINES, A2, P2(a), P2(b), P2(c), P2-CONTRAST,
     A5-REF) is gated on.

### P3. With VARHSYEN set, HSSTRT alone sets where the HSYNC pulse begins

1. **Proposition.** While VARHSYEN and VARBEAMEN are both 1, changing only
   HSSTRT moves the leading edge of the horizontal sync pulse relative to
   the picture and leaves its trailing edge in place, in every line. The
   test grades the move of the landmark's distance to each edge, in the
   picture-relative frame of point 7; that the moved edge is the leading one
   is not shown (point 7, "Stated limit"). p.280,
   with p.298, p.304 and p.305; `opus:8-appA-oos.c5` bundles this with
   address, write-only, Agnus and ECS-mark clauses section 1 declines, so no
   id is carried here.
2. **Stimulus.** Program S, runs SA (s = $0010) and SB (s = $0020), both
   with e = $0030 and B = $01A0.
3. **Observation.** The video output's horizontal sync and R, G, B.
   - Graded output lines are those between the second and third output
     VSYNC pulses after MARK1 that contain a white pixel (R = G = B = 15).
     VARHSYEN has been 1 since before MARK1, and a whole field of margin
     covers the line doubler's delay.
   - In each graded line, three positions are measured in output pixel
     clocks: the leading edge L of the HSYNC pulse, its trailing edge T, and
     the landmark M, the first white pixel after L.
   - The graded distances are dL = M − L and dT = M − T.
4. **Expected.**
   - (a) Within each run, dL and dT are exactly the same in every graded
     line (no tolerance band: the pages give none). One line per run,
     P3/P4(a) [SA], [SB], [SC], [SA0], [SB0], [SC0], shared with P4; P3 uses
     the first and second, and the contrast runs SA0 and SB0.
   - (b) dL(SB) ≠ dL(SA): exact inequality. (P3(b).)
   - (c) dT(SB) = dT(SA): exact equality. (P3(c).)
   - Contrast: with VARHSYEN = 0, dL(SB0) = dL(SA0) and dT(SB0) = dT(SA0).
     (P3-CONTRAST, point 6.)
   - The conjunction line P3 is the whole proposition: (a) for SA, SB, SA0
     and SB0, (b), (c) and the contrast hold together in one parity class.

   dL(SA), dL(SB), dT(SA) and dT(SB) in (b) and (c) are each the value (a)
   establishes as the same in every line of that run's class (named as the
   class's median, rather than a single line's own reading); P3(b), P3(c)
   and P3-CONTRAST are therefore gated on the (a) lines of the runs they
   compare.

   Sources: "HSSTRT 1DE W A(E) Horizontal sync start (VARHSY)" (p.280),
   "HSSTRT W A Horizontal line position for HSYNC start" (p.304), and "8
   VARHSYEN Variable horizontal sync enable" (p.305). The end of the pulse
   is HSSTOP's: "Horizontal line position for HSYNC stop" (p.280, p.304).
   HSSTOP is unchanged, so its edge stays. VARBEAMEN is also set (page
   note 8): p.305's "7 VARBEAMEN Variable beam counter comparator enable"
   names no comparator it excludes, and HSSTRT and HSSTOP are themselves
   positions compared with the horizontal beam counter ("Horizontal line
   position for HSYNC start/stop", p.304).

   Neither the direction nor a magnitude of the move is graded, because
   the pages give neither the unit nor the bit layout of HSSTRT (page
   note 7): (b) and (c) grade exactly what the pages state -- that HSSTRT
   moves the leading edge and does not move the trailing edge -- as exact
   inequality and exact equality, not against an invented threshold (no
   ">=4", "<=1" or "+-1" band). The quantifier is "every line": all graded
   lines, exactly 256 per parity class (P3/P4-LINES below). Vertical-blanking
   lines carry no landmark, and the
   pages say nothing about HSYNC during VSYNC.
5. **Preconditions.**
   - PC-ID and PC-MARK of the run.
   - **P3/P4-LINES [SA], [SB], [SC], [SA0], [SB0], [SC0]:** exactly 256
     graded output lines in each parity class (512 total; section 7's
     "Doubled lines") in the run. One line is one output line carrying
     white; its first white span is its landmark.
   - **Anchor A4**, below, on all six runs: it gates P3/P4(a), (b), (c), the
     contrasts and the conjunctions.
6. **Contrasting cases.**
   - Runs SA0 and SB0 (VARBEAMEN = 1, VARHSYEN = 0, all else equal). The
     page ties HSSTRT to "(VARHSY)", so with the enable clear, the HSSTRT
     value must not move either edge: dL(SB0) = dL(SA0) and
     dT(SB0) = dT(SA0), both exact equality (P3-CONTRAST).
   - For the preconditions: the X-NOBPL runs fail P3/P4-LINES, and X-OCS,
     X-NTSC and X-HALT-S fail the shared ones.
7. **Reference frame.** The output line runs from one HSYNC leading edge to
   the next.
   - The pulse level is determined in each run as the HSYNC level held for
     less than half of the graded field, measured once over the field (the
     polarity is a property of the run, not of one line). HSYTRUE is 0 in
     every run.
   - L is the transition into that level, and T is the first transition out
     of it after L.
   - The landmark is the black-to-white edge at data word 14. It is fixed in
     the chip's line by DIWSTRT, DDFSTRT and the data (p.58 to p.60), none of
     which the sync registers touch.
   - **Anchor A4:** every graded line has exactly one transition into white
     and one out of white, and a first white span whose length is *exactly*
     the same in every line of all six S runs, in both parity classes. The
     pages give no tolerance band for this width, so none is invented; the
     measured population (section 7, Measurements) shows the same width in
     every one of the 512 lines of every run. A4 reads the white
     transitions and the width only, not dL or dT, so it does not depend on
     clause (a) or any other clause it gates. (A two-level sync signal has
     exactly one transition out of the pulse between two consecutive
     transitions into it, so a count of trailing edges is not a clause.)
     Two contrasts, each breaking one clause: X-A4DOUBLE gives every line a
     second white span (the first as wide as the normal one), X-A4WIDTH
     gives every line one white span a data word narrower.
   - **Anchor A5-REF:** in SA0, SB0 and SC0, the accesses this test selects
     as memory refresh -- the reads of Chip word 0 (a selection rule of the
     test, which the anchor checks against the pages) -- behave as the pages'
     refresh slots do: groups (neighbours less than 32 color clocks apart,
     as in P2) of exactly 4 accesses (p.194: "4 cycles for memory refresh";
     p.285: "four refresh time slots"), at least 256 of them in the graded
     field, and the first accesses of consecutive groups 227 or 228 color
     clocks apart (p.23: "all 227 long"; p.304: 227.5 on average, the S
     runs leaving the long-line toggle on). It consumes kCck and is gated on
     both A3 lines. Contrast: X-A5REF starts audio channel 0 DMA on a
     one-word buffer at Chip address 0, adding reads of word 0 to the groups.
   - **Anchor A5:** the landmark M's own distance to R, the nearest
     memory-refresh access before it (the reference: p.194, p.285) -- a
     landmark independent of DIWSTRT, DDFSTRT, HSSTRT and HSSTOP alike.
     Graded on the three VARHSYEN = 0 contrast runs, where nothing is
     expected to move (p.280 "(VARHSY)", p.305 VARHSYEN): M's distance to R
     must be exactly the same in SA0, SB0 and SC0, in both parity classes.
     A5 is gated on A5-REF and on P3/P4(a) of its three runs (a landmark
     that moves relative to the sync edges within a run is reported once, by
     that line). This confirms the apparatus introduces no spurious shift of
     its own when the chip's own signals do not move. With VARHSYEN = 1 (the
     live SA/SB/SC runs), M's distance to R is measured and printed but not
     graded: it changes between SA, SB and SC even though DIWSTRT and
     DDFSTRT (hence M's own chip-side position) are unchanged, because the
     video output the test sees has passed a line doubler that re-times the
     chip's lines. A5 therefore cannot attribute the move of (b) and (c) to
     the leading edge specifically rather than to the picture; it
     establishes only that the apparatus itself is exact when nothing moves.
     Contrast: the lines of the X-SHIFT-SB0 capture in place of SB0's. Its
     landmark sits one data word earlier in half of its lines (p.58 to
     p.60), so M's distance to R is no longer the same within a class and A5
     goes red.
   - **Stated limit.** P3 and P4 are graded in the picture-relative frame:
     dL and dT are distances from the sync edges to the landmark M, and a
     change of HSSTRT or HSSTOP shows as a change of one distance with
     the other exactly unchanged. Which edge moved is not shown: the
     line doubler re-times the chip's lines, so no reference fixed in the
     chip's line (A5's refresh access is the page-anchored candidate)
     survives to the boundary the test sees. The labels of P3 and P4 and a
     RECORDED line say so.

   **Doubled lines.** The line doubler emits two output lines per chip
   line, and nothing on the pages covers the second. The graded lines are
   therefore split into two classes by the parity of their index within the
   graded field. P3/P4(a) is graded within each class; (b), (c) and the
   contrast are graded class by class, comparing the same class across runs.
   Each of these lines is green when it holds in at least one class; the
   conjunction P3 or P4 is green when all of them hold together in one
   class, and red otherwise. A4 and A5 are exact invariants of the
   apparatus and are graded in both classes.

   - (b) dT(SC) ≠ dT(SA): exact inequality. (P4(b).)
